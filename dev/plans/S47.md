# S47 — notes

Role lines and the reasoning behind the steps of `PLAN.md`'s S47. The steps
themselves stay in the plan (rule 23.1.3); this file is deleted with the stage.

## Critic 2026-09-13, over the breakdown

One round over the five-step breakdown of 2026-09-12, before S47.0 (rule 23.3).
Nine findings: eight accepted, one rejected, and the breakdown went to eight
steps. Edmond agreed the new structure the same day.

**S47.0's candidate set was a false statement about the ruling.** It named
whole-arena retention as the only answer the no-panic ruling admits, while the
stage's own precedent already implements two others: `reset_window` answers a
refused promotion edge by retaining one round's COW children, and
`retained::register` publishes a count without a list for a refused placement.
Accepted; S47.0 now prices four candidates.

**A refused `at` capture loses a term, not a record.** The reconciliation is
`edges_at_promotion + (now − at) − K`, so a refused edge costs one `+1` and a
refused decrement one `−1`, each a bounded leak. A lost `at` drops the whole
delta and can settle a COW survivor low, which frees it early. Accepted: S47.0
prices this refusal on its own, and S47.7 carries its arm.

**`pinned` is an enumerator, not a membership test.** The loop past
`finish_reset` spends the reset's own pin on every block it pinned, and a
header bit answers which block is pinned rather than which blocks are. With a
bit alone, a block pinned for a payload keeps a count nobody releases and never
returns to the pool. Accepted: S47.4 links the pinned blocks into a chain.

**`placed` carries the list address across a deliberate split.**
`place_survivor_lists` places every list before it reads any block's count,
because a holder whose own survivors all died would otherwise report itself
empty before a later block's list landed in its tail. Deleting `placed` without
naming where the list address stands leaves the second pass nothing to publish,
and collapsing the two passes restores the bug the split exists to prevent.
Accepted: S47.5 parks the address in a word of the collector line and leaves
publication to `register`.

**Grouping by sorting the survivor chain reclassifies a survivor.** Deriving a
block from an address is arithmetic and stays legal for a dead survivor, but
excluding the survivors that had a block of their own is a kind load on a block
already handed to the system (`dev/DECISIONS.md`, "Promotion classifies once").
Sorting a segment chain is also not a slice sort: it needs an algorithm and
scratch, and the cost lands on every request reset. Accepted: S47.6 records the
exclusion at promotion time by appending only shared-block survivors to a second
chain, and owes the sort's algorithm, its scratch and a measurement.

**`cow_at_promotion` was claimed by two steps, and S47.2 carried a
stage-level criterion.** Its "global allocations go to 0" cannot hold while
`retained`, `by_block`, `placed` and `settled` are still in the code. Accepted:
`cow_at_promotion` belongs to S47.7 alone, and S47.2's criterion covers its own
two sites.

**S47.1 read two ways, and its criterion was unverifiable.** "Hands over the
log" means either detaching the chain or lending a view of it; the lending
reading is unsound, because `run_user_destructor` appends to the same log from
inside the walk. Checked against `arena.rs`: `drain_destructors`,
`drain_escapees` and `drain_release_log` already take the head and null the
field, and the segments are the arena's own blocks, which `finish_reset`
returns — so detaching owes nothing back. Accepted: the step says detach, names
`arena.rs` in its scope, and takes the Miri slice as its criterion instead of
"the H5 rule holds by reading".

**`retained.len()` is a cardinality a kind stamp cannot answer.** It is the
third operand of `KIND_ARENA_RESET_END`. Accepted: S47.3 keeps a counter.

**Rejected: that a block can enter a reset already stamped
`BLOCK_KIND_RETAINED`.** `Arena::fresh_block` stamps `BLOCK_KIND_ARENA` through
`store_block_kind` on every block it draws from the pool, and
`alloc_preferring`'s third arm reaches the pool only through `alloc`, so the
stamp answers "retained by this reset" exactly.

**The stage's own "Done when" was wider than its steps.** The arena's escapee,
destructor and release logs are appended to during a reset and are no step's
subject; their segments are arena blocks rather than global-allocator memory,
so the criterion names `promote`'s path.

## Critic 2026-09-13, over the first answer to S47.0

The first answer — every refused draw abandons the reset and leaves every block
of the arena retained — was refused by the Critic before the step closed, and
the entry it stood in was taken out of `dev/DECISIONS.md` rather than
superseded there: it never governed any code. Nine findings, all accepted. What
they settle stands below; what they open is Edmond's, and S47.0 stays open for
it.

**Retention has no mechanism, and the nearest thing to one frees what it was
to keep.** `arena_reset_full` takes `*mut Arena` and the arena outlives the
call, so returning early leaves `blocks`, `bump` and `limit` untouched: the
next allocation on that arena writes over a promoted survivor, and
`Arena::drop` reaches `finish_reset(|_| false)` and hands every block to the
pool. Calling `finish_reset(|_| true)` instead is worse — it asserts the logs
are drained, which a refusal leaves them not, and it frees the `larges` log,
whose runs are exactly the unpromoted entities a promoted survivor still names.

**A block abandoned without `register` underflows its count word.** A survivor
promoted before the refusal is published in the heap, so its death arrives
later and from outside, reaching `occupant_freed` on a block whose count word
is zero: a `debug_assert` in a debug build, and in a release build the
subtraction wraps and the block is held for ever. Abandonment therefore needs a
block kind of its own, whose `ll_free` arm discards the free and whose
collector arm reads no occupant index.

**Three of the four draws need no refusal arm, because they need no draw.**
`mark_subgraph`'s `stack` duplicates the survivor chain: `mark_one` appends to
both, and an index walk over the chain reaches the same closure, which is the
walk `retrace_survivors` already runs for the same reason. The grouping is the
chain sorted by the 64 KiB mask, with the survivors that had a block of their
own partitioned out at the instant promotion classifies them, so no byte is
drawn and nothing is classified twice. And "retained in this reset" is the
block's own kind word, since `Arena::fresh_block` stamps `BLOCK_KIND_ARENA` on
every block it draws — which also answers the trap that a sweep restamping
retained blocks would zero the pins already placed on them, `retain_block`
being once per block per reset.

**The refusal condition is not the out-of-memory floor.** A segment is drawn
through `stdapi::ll_alloc` at 4 KiB, a small size class of the thread's heap,
and the block pool being empty is a different event; the tests reach the arm
through `RefusedSegments` rather than through pressure. So the argument that
the leak is paid when the process is out of memory anyway does not hold, and
the leak compounds: each refusal costs a request's arena, and the loss makes
the next refusal likelier.

**The `at` capture has two refusal shapes with opposite directions.** Reading a
lost `at` as zero settles the child high, which leaks. Losing the whole pair
drops the survivor from `at_promotion`, leaving `now` in the header, which is
low by `edges − at` whenever a later round found holders — an early free. Which
one a refusal produces depends on whether the segments carry pairs or values,
and the step that builds it owes that in words.

**Smaller, and accepted:** the entry's claim that a block missing from the
grouping is not retained is false against today's code, where `retained`
decides retention and `by_block` decides only the list; the window's guard
frees deferred large bodies on its way out, so "nothing is freed" is not
literal; skipping `weak::drain_arena_weak_log` leaves a weak reference held by
a heap object resolving into the previous request's arena; and the cost quoted
against `at = 0` as a second arm was overstated, `reset_window` already
carrying two different arms for its two record kinds.

**What is left open.** After the three draws go, the reset still draws the
survivor chain, and the COW capture is either a draw or a log record. A
refused chain segment has no local compensation — the survivor's children are
never traced, so the arena frees them under a promoted holder — and the
alternatives are a new block kind with the abandonment machinery above, or a
reserve the chain cannot exhaust, which `memory::reserve` is not: it holds two
blocks per thread, sized from the poll contract, while the chain grows with the
survivor count.

## Sage 2026-09-13, on the arm for the survivor chain. Final

Edmond sent the branch to the Sage rather than choosing between the three
prices. The ruling is `dev/DECISIONS.md`, "a survivor cell the pool cannot
supply severs the edge, and the reset finishes", and it refuses all three
candidates.

Abandonment is unsound rather than dear, which both Critic rounds missed: an
abandoned arena leaves `RequestArena` entities reachable from the heap, the
program stores into them in later requests, and an arena-to-arena store is not
an escape the barrier records — so the next reset frees what the abandoned
holder names. Drawing from the arena's blocks is adopted, as a corollary rather
than as the arm. Reporting outward was never the arm: `rfc/runtime/exceptions.md`
already has `ll_arena_reset` on the pending list, so the claim that the reset
has no caller to report to was false against the specification, and the report
says what the arm cost rather than standing in for it.

Checked against the tree before the ruling was filed: `exceptions.md` carries
the gap almost verbatim — the fixpoint's working memory "is funded by none of
the three reserves", and "finishes the reset" currently assumes that working
memory is available — and the enumeration row for `ll_arena_reset` reads "on
the list"; `cycle::collect` refuses to open while the reset window is open
(`collect.rs:101`). One thing the ruling costs less than it says:
`cells::Cell` already carries the cell's address and its shape beside the
child, and `cells::empty_cell` already writes the null, both built for the
collector's sever, so S47.2 owes a promotion-facing form of `trace_cells`
rather than new machinery.

## Critic 2026-09-13, over S47.1's change

Four findings, all accepted.

The comment justifying the in-place walk cited a guard that does not exist:
`memory::gc_metadata`'s ownership assert is about a block the collector adopts
and sees neither arena nor reset. The probe that aborted there on 2026-09-13
hit it by accident, so the sentence promised a refusal the crate does not
make. The probe itself was the error: a `__destruct` body reaches the runtime
to allocate, to log an escape and to track a destructor

A `DetachedLog` could be taken and dropped unwalked, losing every record, and
`finish_reset`'s "logs must be drained" assert could no longer report it: that
assert reads the arena's fields, which the take has already nulled. The type
carries `#[must_use]`, `for_each` takes the head out, and the drop asserts the
head is null; `arena::tests::the_logs_the_reset_reads::`
`a_detached_log_nobody_walks_is_reported` is the test, seen red for that

Two wordings were wrong. Only `forget_large` writes a zero record and only
into `larges`, which `finish_reset` walks itself, so the three logs handed over
this way hold none — the doc said the opposite. And `drain_*` was described as

## Critic and Sage 2026-09-13, over S47.2

Six findings; five taken into the step, and the sixth moved the ruling.

**The sever was written for one layout and applied to four.**
`cells::empty_cell` is the writer for an object's body cell, a `Reference`'s
`Value` and a vector element. A hash entry's element takes a whole-`Value`
store over the entry's collision link, where zero is a legal entry index
rather than an end of chain, and its key word's null reads as an integer key
rather than a hole; a class's outside cells are the group's. The crate says
all three in its own comments at `cells::sever_cells`, `object::`
`for_each_body_cell` and `array::entry::Entry::element`. The Sage ruled the
per-layout sever, which is `dev/DECISIONS.md`, "a sever takes the smallest
unit its holder's layout leaves consistent, and never lands on a counted
edge", and it is S47.8. Until that lands the refusal asserts on those two
shapes rather than corrupting a survivor about to be promoted.

The same ruling gave S47.2 the rule that made the sever's debt provably zero:
an unmarked child carrying `IS_ESCAPEE` has a record the next admission will
consume, so `mark_child` neither pushes nor severs it. A refusal then falls
only on a non-escapee arena child, which the barrier can only leave in a
`RequestArena` holder — one the counting pass has not reached.

**The count is edges, not children.** Two survivors naming one unrecorded
child are two severances, and a child severed from every holder can be
escaped afresh by a later destructor round and promoted after all. Both
contracts say so now.

**A walk made over an empty chain cannot resume**, having no segment to
resume from. The first repair gave it a pointer to the arena's head field,
which Miri rejected on the spot: the next `push_survivor` takes `&mut Arena`
and retags it. The walk holds nothing of the arena's struct now, and
`descend_from` re-asks at its own index when a walk runs dry — one re-ask per
drained walk.

**The chain's direction had no test.** Reversing the `Log::Survivors` link in
`grow_log` left every test in the crate green: nothing produced more than 500
survivors in one reset. `the_survivor_chain_grows_at_its_tail` is the mirror
of `escape_log_survives_segment_growth`, and it is red under the reversal.

Three stale comments went with the change: the newest-first claim on
`LOG_SEG_RECORDS`, which five of six logs now keep; `promote`'s module doc,
which named `trace_entity` as the one tracer for all three traversals; and
`reset_window`'s citation of `promote::mark_one`, now `mark_child`.

One aside the Critic left, not yet built: `count_children` asserts
`traceable_in_full`, which omits `EntityKind::Box`, while `mark_child` admits
any arena child. Whether an arena-category Box is producible is unchecked.

## Critic 2026-09-13, over S47.8's change

Six findings, five accepted and the sixth ruled on by the Sage below.

**The `Outside` shape was an obligation on every class, and this change
shipped a fixture that got it wrong.** `counted_box_cell` answers `Box`, so
`Box` is what a group's `walk_plain` gets for free and `Outside` was a line
each implementer had to remember; `cycle::reclamation`'s
`WALKS_MORE_THAN_IT_SEVERS` already yielded `Box` for a cell outside the
body. A map whose entry sits in outside storage would then have taken
`empty_cell`'s whole-`Value` store over its collision link — the corruption
`Element` exists to prevent, through the door beside it. Accepted:
`PlainCells::walk_outside` stamps every cell it forwards, and the class
stamps nothing.

**The string-key case turned on a per-process seed.** A fresh table is
neither reseeded nor strong, so a string's slot is `hash_bytes` under the
OS-drawn seed, and three literal keys shared a bucket about once in 256
runs — the hole's kept link, the one thing that separates `sever_entry`
from `remove`, was unmeasured in the rest. Accepted: the colliding key is
found by search over `hash_bytes` under the table's own mask, and the chain
length is asserted before the reset, as the integer case already did.

**The element beside a refused key reached `displaced` with no witness.**
Deleting that branch left the suite green. Accepted: two cases now follow
the collateral — one where it is an arena child admitted a moment earlier,
whose count is rebuilt from the edges that remain, and the Sage's below.

**The count and the journal say different things now**, and
`arena_reset_full`'s contract still said they were one number. Accepted as
a wording repair: the return counts refusals, the journal records every
emptied edge, and a record cannot say which of an entry's two children was
the refused one. Distinguishing them needs a record kind of its own and no
reader has asked for it.

**`empty_cell`'s arm for the three foreign shapes was a `debug_assert`**,
so in release it wrote nothing while its callers handed the child on as
displaced — a dangling slot rather than the missed collection an empty
fall-through usually costs. Accepted: it panics in every build.

**The outside-cell case could not see a `sever_one` that ignored its
cell**: the refused child sat in the first cell, so emptying the block from
the front passed. Accepted: the refused child moved to the second cell.

## Sage 2026-09-13, on the entry unit and the escapee beside it. Final

The Critic's sixth: a string key's unit is the whole entry, so the element
beside it loses its edge too, and that element may carry `IS_ESCAPEE` —
which is what `mark_child`'s per-cell rule exists to keep a sever away
from.

**The edge is not a counted edge, and the reset does nothing extra.** The
store of an escapee `C` into an arena array deposited nothing in `C`'s
count word: `publish_child` calls `ll_retain`, which returns before the
counter for an arena non-COW entity, and `store_category_barrier`'s two
arms both test a category mismatch that `RequestArena → RequestArena` does
not make. `C`'s count is `escape_gain`'s alone, `mark_root` keeps it, and
the one place `A`'s edge would have become a count — `count_children`'s
`+1` — runs after the sever and reads a hole.

**The invariant is the holder's category, not the child's flag.** A sever
lands only on a holder still `RequestArena`; a store into such a holder
counts nothing into an arena child, records a heap child's release at the
reset, and never gains an escape. That one precondition covers every
displaced child, where a check on the child would say nothing about a heap
or COW collateral. The step takes a `debug_assert` on the holder's category
in `sever_one_edge` and the case
`a_refused_key_severs_an_entry_whose_element_escaped`. A `debug_assert`
rather than an `assert`: a violation is a bounded leak, never an early
free, and this path exists to survive a memory refusal rather than to end
the request on one.

**Refused:** refusing to sever a key whose element is an escapee, there
being nothing else to sever and no counted edge to protect; a compensating
adjustment for the collateral, which would be the phantom count itself; a
third key sentinel that would let the key be emptied while the element
stands, which buys a preserved uncounted edge at the price of a key state
every lookup, the tracer and the compaction must learn.

## Critic 2026-09-13, over S47.4's change

Five findings, all accepted. The chain itself the Critic could not break:
it walked the four failure classes — a second payload in one block, a
chain member reaching the pool early, a double `ll_free`, a non-terminating
walk — and each is closed. One of the reasons was unstated and is now in
the code: `register` runs before the walk, so it always sees the reset's
hold and answers false for a chain member; put the walk above
`place_survivor_lists` and the same block is freed twice.

**The collector line has sixteen bytes left, and the next two steps want
four words.** Its fields sum to 48 of the 64 the const assert allows, and a
49th byte makes the struct 128 and overflows the block's header line. The
budget and the way out — one word carrying both the pin chain and S47.5's
emptied chain, the two never claiming it at the same instant — are now a
`note:` on S47.5, with S47.6 pointed at it.

**The field's doc named a guard that does not hold.** It said no other
reader exists because a collection cannot open while a reset window is
open; `reset_window::is_open` is a thread-local, so another thread's
collection may read this block's line at the same time. What actually keeps
the word private is that no code outside `arena_reset_full` writes it, and
that is what the doc says now.

**A zero link would have been followed.** The walk stopped only on the end
sentinel, so a corrupted link sent it to a collector line at address zero —
which is how the mutation of the membership test failed: a segfault with no
sentence. It now names that state before it can be followed.

**The new test could not see the reset's own pin.** Every outcome it read —
the kind, the payload pins, the block going home — is the same whether the
reset took one pin, two or none, and deleting the whole arm left it green.
The walk counts what it spends now (`promote::take_pins_spent`), and the
test asserts one; the deletion is red against that count.

**The criterion reads two ways.** There are two `retained::pin` sites, and
only the refused payload's belongs on the chain: a survivor list's pin is
spent by `release_emptied` when the block the list belongs to returns.
Chaining that one too would hand a holder block to the pool with a live
list still standing in it. The site now says so, and the handoff names
which pin the criterion means.

**Left as a probe, not a finding:** whether an occupant of a retained block
can be freed from another thread inside the reset that retained it, which
would borrow out of the pins in the count word. It is in the plan's Fog.

## Critic 2026-09-13, over S47.5's change

Four findings, all accepted; the first reversed a decision the step had
made on arithmetic that did not hold.

**The budget that justified one word for two chains was wrong.**
`BlockCollector` is `align(64)`, so its size was already 64 with 48 bytes
of fields: three eight-byte words and the header's own six fill the line
exactly, and a separate word for the emptied chain costs nothing. What the
sharing bought was the expensive half — "read the link out before clearing
it", "pins are only taken during promotion", and two assertions that could
name only one of the two states, because both were one non-zero. The
chains have a word each now, `reset_pins` and `emptied_chain`, and
`link_emptied` asserts both. The line is full: S47.6 reuses a word or
shrinks the header, and its `note:` says so.

**The guard test missed the function that writes all three words.**
`memory::heap::clear_collector_line` is `pub(crate)` and zeroes the line;
`memory::retained::commission_retained_block` calls it. Reaching a block
the reset has already chained would take it off the chain with the chain
still naming it — in a debug build the assertion would blame the block
before it, and in a release build the walk would stop and every block
behind it would keep its pin for the life of the process. Today's callers
are the reset and a test helper over a block fresh from the pool, so the
test names the clearing with its own list of files rather than forbidding
it.

**The refused placement had no test and three branches standing on it.**
`alloc_preferring` answers null only when the pool has nothing left, so
the sentinel the placing pass writes, its reading by the publishing pass
and `register`'s own null arm would all have run for the first time
together, under a real exhaustion.
`memory::arena::RefusedListPlacement` injects the refusal and
`a_refused_placement_publishes_the_count_without_a_list` is the case: the
survivor is promoted, the block holds its occupant with no list, and it
goes home on that occupant's death. The zero that means "never in the
pass" now takes the same arm as the sentinel in both builds, by an
explicit test rather than by the accident that they decode alike.

**`retain_block`'s argument covered two of its three populations.** The
block holding a refused payload is a buffer-arena chunk's, which
`Arena::fresh_block` never stamped, so the sentence about `BLOCK_KIND_ARENA`
does not reach it. The contract now names that population and its own
reason: the only writer of `BLOCK_KIND_RETAINED` is `retain_block`, and a
block that carried the stamp left its last reset through `ll_free`, which
hands it to the pool and `BLOCK_KIND_FREE`.

**What the Critic could not break**: the claim that no block is on both
chains, which it checked against three orders — a block pinned for a
payload that also holds survivors, a list placed into a pinned block, and
a block whose occupants all die inside the reset. In each, `register`
answers false because the reset's own pin is unspent.

## S47.6 — the baseline the step erases, and the design

Taken on `7b5c379` before the first code edit, as the plan's review gate asks.

**Manager-allocation budget.** `by_block` draws from the process allocator and
from nothing else: 4 calls for three blocks of three survivors each, 11 for
eight blocks of three, which is one table plus one vector per block and the
table's own growth. Measured by
`where_a_survivor_list_is_placed::the_grouping_draws_nothing_from_the_global_allocator`,
red at 4 on that commit.

**Operation count per survivor:** one `SipHash-1-3` of the block address and one
bucket probe, one vector push, and one vector allocation per block; then the map
is iterated twice, once per placement pass. **Cache working set:** the table's
buckets and one heap allocation per block, all of it outside the arena and cold.
**Lifetime:** from the first promotion of the reset to `place_survivor_lists`'s
return, across every destructor round. **Refusal model:** none — a table growth
the allocator refuses ends the process, which is the debt the stage pays
(`dev/DECISIONS.md`, "no runtime path may panic on allocation").

**The clock, on the same commit and the same box**, from
`promote::tests::what_the_grouping_costs::measure_the_grouping` in release, the
minimum of 15 rounds and four runs of each shape: 2400 survivors over 20 blocks
54.9, 55.6, 63.9 and 87.1 µs; over 2 blocks 50.3, 50.8, 53.0 and 53.0 µs. The
figures after the change and what they can and cannot be read as are
`dev/BENCHMARKS.md`, 2026-09-13, "S47.6 the reset's grouping leaves the global
allocator".

**The design.** The map's key is an address masked to 64 KiB and its value is a
list; the block that key names has a collector line of its own, cleared by
`retain_block` a moment earlier, so the line is the map. Three walks of the
survivor chain replace it:

1. **Count.** Per survivor, `survivor_count` of its block's line gains one and
   `held_occupants` gains one if the slot still holds an allocation identity.
   Both are `retained`'s, which owns that rule.
2. **Place and fill.** Per survivor, a block whose `placed_list` is still zero
   has its list placed now — `alloc_preferring` as before, its holder retained
   and pinned as before — and the word takes the list's address; then the
   survivor's address is written at that address and the word advances past it.
   So `placed_list` is the fill cursor, and the publish pass recovers the head
   by subtracting `survivor_count` words from it.
3. **Publish.** Per survivor, a block whose `placed_list` is non-zero is
   published: the word is taken, the head recovered, `register` sorts the list
   in place and adds the held count, and a block nothing holds joins the
   emptied chain. Every later survivor of that block reads zero and skips.

The split the Critic of 2026-09-13 defended survives by construction: pass 3 is
a walk of its own, so every list is placed before any block's count is read.

**The chain is not sorted, and the step's `done:` said it would be.** A
comparison sort of a segment chain needs random access by index, which costs a
segment walk per access, and it buys nothing: the addresses have to end up in
the per-block lists, so a distribution keyed by the block writes them where they
belong in one pass instead of permuting them where they stand and copying them
afterwards. The only sort left is the one `register` already makes, a
`sort_unstable` per list — pattern-defeating quicksort, in place and
allocation-free — which is what the trace's binary search needs. The step's
clause is amended to say so.

**A survivor that had a block of its own is partitioned out by a tag on its
chain record**, set by promotion at the instant it classifies. Zeroing the record
was refused: the re-trace of a later round and the count pass both read every
survivor, a promoted large entity included. `Arena::walk_survivors` masks the
tag, so every existing pass is unchanged, and the grouping walks the records
that carry none. Asking again in the grouping is what
`dev/DECISIONS.md`'s "Promotion classifies once" forbids, and what the block
itself makes expensive to get wrong: a large entity's run carries a
`LargeEntityHeader`, whose offset 16 is the mapped length `free` unmaps where an
arena block keeps the bump fill `alloc_preferring` reads. The run is mapped and
stamped for the whole reset — `reset_window::defer_free` holds back a large
entity's free while a window is open — so the cost of a second classification is
a corrupted run rather than a read of returned memory, which is what the first
draft of this section claimed.

**The line has room for the one new word.** `BlockCollector` is 64 bytes with a
four-byte hole at +36, where `held_occupants` lands; the count of all occupants
is `survivor_count`, the field that carries the same number for the list's life,
and a reader cannot see it early because `block_survivor_list` acquire-loads the
address first and answers `(null, 0)` while it is null.

## Critic 2026-09-13, over S47.6's design, before the code

Six findings, all accepted; two of them together moved where the grouping's
numbers live.

**`placed_list` as a moving fill cursor had no arm for a refused placement,
and a second reading of it had none either.** The word carries three states —
not in the pass, refused, an address — and a cursor makes a fourth; the
refusal would have written an entity address through the sentinel, and the
publishing pass, reading the word once per survivor, would have tripped the
accessor's own assert on the second survivor of every block. Accepted: the
word keeps exactly the meaning it had, and the fill index moved into the four
bytes the line had spare.

**The list's head was to be recovered by subtracting a separately maintained
count from that cursor**, which made two numbers from two walks agree with
nothing comparing them, and left the fill unbounded. Accepted with the same
repair: the head is never thrown away, the fill index is checked against the
count before each write, and `register` asserts that the two passes accounted
for the same population.

**Counting the held occupants in the first pass put the whole placement pass
between the occupancy read and the increment it feeds**, and that pass can
draw a block from the pool. Accepted: the holds are counted at publication off
the filled list, which is one statement from the increment, as they were
before the change.

**The grouping's order stops being a `HashMap`'s and becomes the chain's, and
the test guarding the two-pass split could no longer fail.** Its sixteen
rounds existed to sample both orders of two blocks; the chain's order is the
escapee log's, which in that fixture is the safe one. Accepted: the fixture
takes the escape order as a parameter, that test escapes the current block's
survivor first, and it asserts which block the placement pass reached first —
so the collapse fails there rather than passing by luck.

**The tag had no named writer**, and the three candidate sites differ in cost:
an index lookup walks the chain per call, and tagging at push time cannot tag
a root, `admit_escapees_as_survivors`' closure answering a `bool` while the
caller writes the record. Accepted: the walk takes the last record it yielded
out of the grouping, which is O(1) and needs no new type.

**`survivor_count`'s second meaning cannot be fenced by the privacy guard**,
`cycle/row`, `cycle/arena` and `heap`'s enumerator all reading it through
`block_survivor_list`; and a `held_occupants` field would duplicate the low
half of `holds` from the moment the first occupant dies. Accepted: there is no
held field, the guard's own doc carries the reader list and the note that the
line is now full, and the field's doc says what protects the early write.

## Critic 2026-09-13, over S47.6's change

Seven findings: five accepted, one refused with its reason recorded in the
plan's Fog, one answered as an open question already there.

**The refused arm had no population-agreement assert, and a miscount there is
unrecoverable.** With the fill pass counting only held occupants for a
listless block, a survivor it skipped left the count one short, and the next
death of the block's last counted occupant returned it to the pool under a
live promoted survivor. Accepted: the refused arm counts every occupant in the
same word and puts each hold straight on the count word as it reads the slot,
the refusal taking a hold of the reset's own so no free can return the block
while those counts arrive, and `register` spends it. The assert now covers
both arms and was seen red on the refused one.

**The reason recorded for taking a large survivor out of the grouping was
refuted by `reset_window::defer_free`**, which holds back a large entity's
free while a window is open: the run is mapped and stamped for the whole
reset, so nothing here reads returned memory. Accepted, and the real hazard is
sharper — a run carries a `LargeEntityHeader`, whose offset 16 is the mapped
length `free` unmaps where an arena block keeps the bump fill
`alloc_preferring` reads, and it has no collector line of the reset's. Three
comments and this file said the wrong thing and now say that one.

**`retain_block`'s "other population" paragraph named a population no producer
reaches.** A carry is refused when the *destination* allocation fails, so the
payload keeps the address it had, and a `RequestArena` entity's body came from
`Arena::alloc_body` — an arena bump block, stamped by `fresh_block` like every
other. Accepted, and it corrects the S47.5 round, whose finding put the false
sentence there: a buffer-arena chunk is never the block a refused carry pins.

**Two sentences of the diff were false**: the line holds three of the
grouping's numbers rather than two, and the claim that `register` asserts the
passes agreed was true only on the placed arm. Accepted; the second is true
now.

**The probe cannot support the comparison the step promises.** Accepted in
part: the before-arm timings are in this file's baseline section now, and the
two shapes' confound — twenty blocks means twenty lists placed outside their
own block and nineteen more blocks returned inside the timed region — is named
in the probe's doc and in `dev/BENCHMARKS.md`. Refused that the `HashMap` arm
must stand in the tree to be measurable: an A/B of a change names the base
commit, which the journal entry does, and keeping a dead arm for a probe is
what `dev/WORKFLOW.md` puts in the archive branch instead.

**Refused: re-ordering the placement tiers** so that a list prefers the tail
of a block the reset is keeping anyway. The tier order is S36.9e's, with tests
of its own, and the Critic's own reading says the trade — pairwise
dependencies between retained blocks — needs pricing. It is in the plan's Fog.

**Answered rather than repaired: the absorb gate is thread-local while
`ll_free` is any-thread.** That is the plan's open question about a free
arriving from another thread inside the reset that retained the block, and
this step neither widens nor narrows the window it needs: the refused arm's
counts land earlier than `register` did, and the placed arm's land at the same
instant. The Fog line now carries the wrapping word as well as the eaten pin.
