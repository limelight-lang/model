# S58 — The exit's own containers: notes

The role lines and the reasoning of `PLAN.md` S58, kept here because the
section would otherwise run past forty lines (`dev/INDEX.md`, Conventions).
Deleted with the stage; a ruling or a measurement goes to the journals first.

## The Critic's pass over the draft, 2026-09-18

The draft ordered the steps registry, teardown sink, arena box. Four
findings, each with what it changed:

1. **The order inverted the goal.** The registry step routes registration
   through `buffer_ensure_longlived`, which on a thread whose arena is
   untouched reaches `with_buffer_arena` and its `Box::new` — the abort the
   stage exists to remove — and the step's red test could not see it, every
   static-block test taking `test_guard`, which warms that `Box` on the test
   thread. Accepted: the arena step is first (S58.1), and its red test runs
   on a thread with no warm-up.
2. **A registration after the pass has drained parked a block for the
   process's life.** Step 2 of the exit runs destructors after step 1 has
   released the registry; a registration there would draw a chunk nothing
   pops, and at step 5 `hand_over` sends its block to the abandoned list,
   where it never empties. Today the same registration leaks a `Vec` on the
   global heap, bytes off every walk. Accepted: `ll_thread_exit` closes the
   registry after the pass and a later registration is refused, which is the
   cost the refusal already names — that block's roots leak. A second static
   pass after step 2 was refused: `heap.rs` fixes step 2 as the last step
   that runs user code.
3. **"Pops pairs off the buffer's end" admitted two readings that break.** A
   pop that writes `len` or the whole `Buffer` back after `tear_down` returns
   clobbers a mid-pass growth (the reallocating path frees the old chunk, and
   the write-back restores `data` to it); and a `len` kept as a pair count
   loses fifteen sixteenths of the pairs at the first reallocation, since
   `buffer_ensure_longlived` copies `len` bytes. Neither is a defect of the
   mechanism; the `done:` now states the pop as one access to the
   thread-local with nothing derived outliving it, and `len` in bytes. What
   the Critic checked and found sound: both release points for the emptied
   chunk; the reallocating path's free of the old chunk, withheld or not,
   touches nothing the pass holds; `try_grow_in_place` moves nothing; a
   refused growth leaves the `Buffer` as it was; a registry past 4,080 blocks
   leaves the arena for an OS-direct run and still frees by kind.
4. **The red-test figures.** A first registration makes two global calls
   (the hand-rolled `Registered` box, then `try_reserve(1)` on an empty
   `Vec`), not one; the arena's `dispose` is a `dealloc`, read by the third
   counter. Accepted, the `done:` lines corrected.

On the observable change of S58.3 — a destructor run for slot *i* sees the
slots after *i* still holding their live, counted occupants where today it
reads every slot of the block as null — the Critic found no defect, checking
a double release (one emptier per slot, the pass's or a user store's), a use
after free (a later slot's occupant holds its own count through the earlier
destructor), leaks (a store into a later slot is released under the new form
and rooted forever under the old), the walk's state (immutable runs over a
fixed base, each slot read at visit time) and the cross-block order the two
order cases assert. What the step owes beyond its draft: `sever_counted_slots`
is documented for the sever-all-then-drop shape and loses its only caller,
so it goes rather than keeping a contract nothing uses.

One aside, without a producer: with the arena inline, the sentence "a later
free would build a second arena through the lazy path and leak it" (in
`buffer_arena.rs` and `heap.rs`) is false in both halves — a later free
posts remote, since `hand_over` nulled every owner, and a later *alloc*
bumps a fresh block under the TLS address that nothing hands over. The
Critic found no long-lived payload allocated after step 5 on the production
path. Disposition: the two sentences are rewritten to state the trap; a
fence that answers null after `dispose` is not built, since it would cost a
read on every long-lived allocation for a shape no path has.
