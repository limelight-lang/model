Sage ruling on the recall, second round — Final.

**A. Which takes recall.** Within ruling (2), no take recalls: the three critical paths wait by his ruling, the explicit fire waits for its own batch (first ruling §2, standing), and the poll's arms never meet `COLLECTOR` once E is fixed. So S65.6's recall keeps its readers and loses its take-side writer. The hint stays on the token line and gains three values in its byte, `NONE < ANNOUNCED < RECALLED`: `take_unless` stores `ANNOUNCED` by a swap from `NONE` (a failed swap ignored — Critic F5), the free path at M stores `RECALLED` by `fetch_max` (S65.7), the take's clear is a swap `ANNOUNCED → NONE`, and `RECALLED` clears at the consent before the release swap and on the drain under `FREE`, as the package's section 6 has it. The collector's readers split by value: `recall_stands` — the stride and `grow` — is `RECALLED` alone, so a batch a take announces runs to its end; `serve_the_grant`'s pre-batch check reads `ANNOUNCED` or above and releases with no batch, because a grant not yet batched is a held grant whose release abandons nothing while its mutator stands blocked — (2)'s reason has no work to point at. Count, stride, growth reading and `FinishThePosts` are unchanged. `a_pressure_collection_recalls_the_grant_over` loses its instrument and is rewritten to raise `RECALLED` through the fixture (the five containers keep the stride bound), and a new case holds (2): a pressure take mid-batch waits the batch out and its collection reads P's verdicts.

**B. What each critical path does with the batch, from the code.** The pressure path and the exit already use the verdicts as far as they can: `read_batch` takes R whole and P whole, a P entry is a root only under `Proposed` or `Unwalked` (`Verdict::is_root`), `ReadLive` is skipped and deferred at the close, a completed death retired. No code is owed to "use" the batch after the wait. What the wait buys is exactly the `ReadLive` closures not traced this round and nothing else: a `Proposed` root is validated by an exact trace, which is a full trace (F4), and `Unwalked` roots are traced as R's — S65.10's done-line already keeps them traced under pressure. The teardown retirement reads slot state and never a verdict (`compact` with no prefix frees on `completed_death`), so a completed batch and a recalled one yield it the same slots (F1). The exit's round 1 defers the `ReadLive` roots, `registered_by_lane` counts that move as progress, round 2 re-offers and traces them — `collect_before_exit`'s own comment says so — so the exit gains one round's postponement of the same trace (F1). Change per path: none; the new thing is the cost sheet — pressure gains `ReadLive` once (15 µs on `overlapping-live`), the retirement and the exit gain nothing, and all three now wait a batch whose length is positions, unbounded in time.

**C. The stranger's batch.** (2)'s and (3)'s reason presuppose the batch is the waiter's own; a grant held unserved has no batch, so releasing it at the stride abandons nothing and is the only bound left on a critical wait behind another thread. It tightens (5) rather than colliding: (5)'s "one stranger's batch" was a time claim on the false block-budget premise, and S65.14 replaces it with N positions. The form, with the Critic's corrections taken (F6): the take that meets `COLLECTOR|s` stores `ANNOUNCED` and sets the slot's word; M's store does the same with `RECALLED`, so a mutator freeing behind a stranger's batch is released too; the stride reads the slot word beside the traced token, clears it before the walk, walks the standing list once per set word — O(n) per announce, not per stride, named as the cost — and releases through `release_claim` (the notify under the mutex) every `COLLECTOR|s` byte that is not the traced mutator's and whose hint is set, without `note_released_unserved`. Trigger: every announcing take and M. Bound: N positions, one walk, one release. Within his rulings' reasons; no question.

**D. S65.7.** M stores `RECALLED` and the collector abandons the batch: his 2026-09-23 ruling ("at M the mutator stores `waiting` and carries on"), untouched by (2), which is about takes. Under perf-first it is the cheap direction: the mutator pays nothing at M and, after S65.10, a disposal of K `Unwalked` instead of a trace; it buys the withheld memory back within a stride, and it is what keeps a long grant from turning withholding into a refusal, where (2) would have the mutator wait the very batch that holds its slots. S65.14 before S65.7 stands (M behind a stranger's batch is read only through the slot word).

**E. The poll window.** Real: the reading at gc.rs:288, the returns at 294 re-enter `ll_free` → stdapi.rs:513 → deferred_slot_reuse.rs:806 → consent. The Critic is right that the `Verdicts` arm cannot meet it (a request lands on `FREE` only; `Verdicts` is armed from `POSTED`, which only this thread moves) and that `AllRoots` can, blocking on a whole batch against E9. Fix: make the withheld returns before the byte is read, as `retire_at_the_poll` already orders it — one line moved; red test: a request landed by a hook between two hand-backs of the drain, the poll armed `AllRoots` — red by a wait, green by a deferral. The first ruling's non-blocking take is withdrawn.
