## Verdict

Build D2 in its exact form first: a take (or threshold batch) whose trace meets the budget at root *j* is reset and re-traced over roots 1..*j*−1, those verdicts are posted, and roots *j*..*n* come back `Unwalked`; the mutator's collection over P then treats `Unwalked` as not-a-root and writes it back into R at the close, which is the mutator writing R as it does today, and a hold-line bit stops the collector re-taking a ring whose smallest span still met the budget. This beats a larger budget (D1a) because it recovers everything that fits under any budget rather than moving the cliff, beats the standalone hold-line bit because that bit alone holds the garbage without ever judging it, and beats resuming a trace across rounds because the rows cannot outlive the grant without the mutator withholding returns across it. D3's contention signal is real and cheap on the collector's side, but its premise — that the split loses with no spare core — is asserted by the owner and by one figure on one shape, and it needs its arm before a mode is built on it. The worst case §2.4 describes — K stuck at one and the mutator re-tracing a shared core once per root — is real for a live core whose members are all candidates, which the prune never cuts, and not for an ordinary live core, which the mutator's first collection stamps and both sides then prune; D2 plus D3's unserved-signal counter degrade that case to the in-line design's cost and no lower.

## Weak points the figures show

Each is a regime — the ring's shape, the live share, the thread's state, the budget — and the figure that shows it, with its line in `model/dev/BENCHMARKS.md`.

**W1. A sparse closure past the budget buys nothing at 75–157 µs of collector time.** Regime: 63 roots, each a ring of five members in five distinct blocks (315 blocks, row arrays of 2,080 bytes each — `what_a_take_costs.rs` lines 60–66 and the S40.5 table, class 128), live or dead, `TRACE_BLOCK_BUDGET` 8. The collector's trace is abandoned at the eighth block after 75–157 µs (line 186–187; 63.0–99.5 µs on the garbage shape, line 319), every root is `Unwalked`, and the mutator's collection costs 2,872,618 instructions against 2,853,251 in line (lines 171–172), +0.68 % (line 203). The cliff is a function of row bytes per touched block, not of roots: with `bytes_for(512) = 2,080` and a budget of 56,960 + 8 × 65,280 = 579,200 bytes, about 278 blocks' rows fit, so about 55 of the 63 rings would fit and 8 would not — an estimate from the constants, not a reading.

**W2. Over a dead ring the take is a loss in cycles and wall, at any budget.** Regime: a standing sub-threshold ring whose components are garbage. mixed-0: 369,781 cycles after a take against 265,374 in line (lines 62–63), 39 % more (line 106), 87 µs against 53–56 µs; the census shows why — 63 roots attempted and 378 rows met in both arms (line 84): the verdict is a shortlist the mutator re-derives whole. The crossing where the split starts to pay is between a quarter and a half live (line 105). This is the price of the owner's line (5), exact validation on the owning thread, and no budget moves it.

**W3. What the take leaves the mutator is the part that misses cache.** Regime: any take. All-live take: 57,293 cycles for 15,365 instructions, a fifth of an instruction per cycle (line 168 and the paragraph at 192–199); overlapping garbage: L1D misses 5,298 against 4,512 (lines 50–51), +31 % cycles for +3.4 % instructions with 15 % more misses (line 352). The verdicts and the rows another core just wrote are the transfer cost of the split.

**W4. The collector's time is treated as free.** 20.6–36.1 µs of trace for the 24 µs the mutator does not spend (lines 211–212, "On one core that is a loss"). No arm runs the collector on a core a mutator wants; every take-cost arm retires the collector before the timed collection (the S64.5 entry's closing paragraph). The owner has confirmed this as first-class; it is D3's core below.

**W5. A round that requests a sleeping population pays the cap's tail once per interval.** Regime: 64–1,000 sleeping sub-threshold threads, standing interval 50 ms. Longest round 5.32 ms with the cap against 2.106 s without (lines 412–413); a producer behind them waits 22.70 ms worst against 1.617 s (lines 426–427). Bounded, but "the sleeping threads here stay asleep" (line 448): the population the cap was ruled for, a pool that parks and wakes, is unmeasured.

**W6. `Unwalked` is the mutator collecting its own roots without shortage.** Not a figure but a reading of the mechanism the figures rest on: `Verdict::is_root` is `Proposed | Unwalked` (`verdicts.rs`), so a take past the budget makes the mutator trace roots nobody judged. The owner's line (3) excludes exactly this. D2 below.

**Where the figures are silent** — regimes no arm has measured:

- One root whose closure alone exceeds the budget (the "the heap" regime the K-halving argument rests on: "where one root's closure alone meets it, K halves to one" is asserted in the ruling, not measured).
- The mutator's collection over P with a round in flight beside it (the S64.5 entry says the collector is retired first).
- A pool that parks and wakes between rounds (W5).
- A component partly live — a live core with dead sub-rings hanging off it — the ordinary shape of a long-lived program; every measured ring is all-live or all-dead.
- The memory a standing ring holds across the 4–8 s before its take, and the withheld deaths during a take's window on a freeing mutator (S38.3 gives 2.6 ns per withheld death and 4.2 ns to return it, but under a threshold batch, not a take).
- The row cost at the smallest class: `bytes_for` is 16,408 at 4,080 slots, so the budget holds about 35 such blocks — a closure over 36 blocks of 16-byte objects meets it; no arm builds that shape.
- Throughput with N mutators on N cores and the collector competing (W4).
- A take served at a checkpoint (a sleeper that woke) rather than inside the wait — S51.5 has 3.46 ms for a threshold ring, nothing for a take.
- The split of the mutator's collection into mark+scan, exact validation and teardown: the mix's linearity (294,000–268,000 instructions per sixteen rings, line 97–99) gives the total per dead ring, not its phases; D1c's gain cannot be priced without it.

## D1 — give the collector more to collect

What "more" has to mean is fixed by what the mutator does with each verdict (`compaction.rs::dispose_verdicts`, `Batch::walk_roots`): `ReadLive` is deferred without a walk, `ZeroCount` is retired on one bit re-read, `Proposed` is traced exactly again, validated and torn down, `Unwalked` is traced exactly. So the collector's work is repeated only for garbage cycles, and it is repeated in full: the mark and scan over `PlainCells` are what rediscover the membership the exact validation then reads. Anything that makes the collector do "more" of the same — a larger budget, a sooner take — changes which rings reach the mutator as `Proposed`, not what the mutator does with one.

**D1a. A larger budget.** Mechanism: `budget_for_this_batch()` returns a bigger figure; nothing else moves. It helps only the regime where the closure that would fit at 32 blocks does not at 8 — the sparse rings of W1 and the one-root-past-budget regime. It does not help W2 or W3, and the sparse regime is a row-representation problem in disguise (Part B, item 1). Cost: blocks drawn from `gc_metadata` per batch, up to the budget, 64 KiB each, and on a pool refusal the collector's arena draws its own thread's critical reserve, which no ruling has licensed (`PLAN.md`, "What S49 named and left"); and the window: a mutator that needs its token waits for the whole trace, and every death of a running mutator is withheld for it. The window can be bounded without a new word: the mutator raises its collecting word before it takes the token (`collect.rs` lines 195–209, `set_collecting` then `HeldToken::take`), so the collector can read `is_collecting()` in `TraceScratchArena::grow` — one relaxed load per block drawn, on the writer line the mutator owns — and refuse the growth when a collection is waiting, which is today's abandonment. Hot-path cost on the mutator: none; the store exists. What it changes in the rfc's letter: E10 removed the collector's reading of the collecting word from the handshake; this reading is a hint whose staleness in either direction is safe (a late-seen waiter costs one more block of trace, an early-seen one an `Unwalked` batch), so it does not reopen E10, but the handshake document should say so. The bound the waiter gets is one block's rows: at the 60–95 ns per row the all-live arm implies (20.6–36.1 µs over 381 rows, an estimate by division), a block of 16,000 dense rows is about 1–1.5 ms — longer than today's whole-batch bound on the corpus shape. Verdict: worth having only as the retry policy under D2 (double the budget on a retake up to a cap), never as the primary answer.

**D1b. A sub-threshold take made sooner.** Changes reclaim latency and the number of windows per unit time, not repetition. Under the owner's rule the interval is the collector's clock and 4 s; every take costs the mutator a window and 1,495–1,562 instructions plus the walk of whatever is proposed (line 207). A sooner take of a dead ring is a sooner instance of W2. Refused as a lever on this question.

**D1c. A verdict the mutator can check more cheaply than re-tracing.** The owner names Bacon–Rajan concurrent's Σ/Δ tests over a member list the collector hands over. I have not re-read that paper for this report and cite it only as he names it; the crate's own exact validation already is the Σ-form — it re-reads current fields and checks that every member's count is accounted for by members (`rfc`, "Speculative tracing and exact validation"; `collect.rs::commit`), and its Δ-form is the ownership invariant plus the rfc's argument that an unreachable cycle cannot acquire a new external reference. What the mutator pays that the collector already paid is the *membership*: the mark and scan over the proposed roots. Handing the membership over is sound with respect to current-field reads: an edge into a member from a non-member shows as IN < RC → `ExternallyReferenced`; a member that lost its last external holder since the trace shows the same way or as a completed death (`ZeroCountMember`). Where it is not sound is memory: the list names non-root members, and a non-root member can die by ordinary refcounting between the collector's release and the mutator's collection over P, and its slot be reissued — the candidate bit protects roots only (`stdapi::ll_free`'s candidate arm). A reissued slot read as a member would be validated as one, and a set that happens to balance would be torn down with a live stranger in it. So the list needs the deferral's contract extended from the grant to the mutator's close: returns withheld while the byte reads `POSTED`. Hot-path price: the free path's branch already tests the byte; `COLLECTOR` becomes `COLLECTOR | POSTED` — one compare, no extra load — but every death in the window pays the withholding, 2.6 ns plus 4.2 ns to return later (S38.3, 2026-09-15), and the window grows from one batch to one batch plus the mutator's poll interval. Structural price: the rows live in the collector's arena, one per collector, so each handed-over batch needs its own arena blocks until the mutator's close, and the `block_shadow` pointers on the mutator's blocks (set by `ensure_row`, cleared by `clear_touched_rows`) stay set across the release — the mutator's own pressure collection would meet a block already enrolled in another arena, which today is the "two touched lists" defect the token exists to prevent. This is the "accelerator" question the rfc leaves open rather than refuses ("whether a worker's trace may hold its arena the same way is open"). Gain: the mark+scan share of a dead ring's collection, which no figure separates from the validation and the teardown. Verdict: a premise-level change (Premise changes, P2) with an unmeasured gain; not before the phase split is read.

**D1d. What else.** Two small things inside the algorithm. First, an abandoned trace can still post `ZeroCount` for a root whose count reads zero: `verdict_for` reads `slot_state` before any row, the reading is independent of the trace, and the mutator retires such a root on its completed-free bit with no walk. Today `post_the_verdicts` posts `Unwalked` for all when `complete` is false. Cost: one load per root on the collector, nothing on the mutator. Second, on the threshold path `size_the_next_batch` halves K on a met budget; once D2's prefix exists, K = max(prefix, 1) is the measured fit rather than a guess.

Nim ORC, for the record: its `collectCycles` traces every root to completion with no budget and no resume, drops every root afterwards (`roots.len = 0`), and adapts its trigger — `rootsThreshold` falls to `max(rootsThreshold div 3 * 2, 16)` after an effective collection (`j.freed * 2 >= j.touched`) and grows by half otherwise (tool summary of `orc.nim`, quoting those expressions). The nearest working thing has no partial trace at all; what it has is a trigger that learns from the last collection's yield, which is the one dial this crate has nowhere.

## D2 — do not make the mutator collect what the collector did not judge

**The reading is right.** `Unwalked` roots are traced by the mutator's collection over P exactly as `Proposed` ones are (`Verdict::is_root`, `Batch::walk_roots`), and the collection over P is armed by `POSTED` at the next poll or slot free with no shortage anywhere. A `Proposed` root's re-trace is the exact validation the owner's line (5) demands; an `Unwalked` root's re-trace is the mutator collecting roots nobody judged, which his line (3) permits only under shortage. D2 is consistency with the algorithm as stated, and the ruling's "Premises" sentence — that the collection over P, `Unwalked` included, is "the accepted mechanism of every batch rather than the mutator collecting its roots of its own accord" — is true of `Proposed` and false of `Unwalked`.

**Does the "shift, not a second trace" accounting survive?** No, on both thread kinds. The ruling's alternative is "the X collection over R whole four seconds later". That collection is armed by `reoffer_deferred_if_epoch_moved` at a poll, which fires only when the deferred lane is occupied and the epoch turned; the collector's turnover ask is answered by the poll only `if open && deferred_lane_is_occupied()` (`gc.rs` lines 265–270). A thread with an empty lane never collects R whole on its own — its standing ring waits for the take, the threshold, pressure or exit. So for a lane-less thread the unwalked take is not a shift but a walk that would otherwise never have happened; and after that walk the live roots go to the lane, so the thread now has one and re-walks them every X (S60's accepted price for live deferred roots). For a lane-holding thread the X collection reads R whole either way, so the take *adds* one exact walk of the live closures, it does not move one. The accounting was written against the take succeeding, and it does not survive the alternative the owner names.

**The ruling's reasons, one by one.** (i) Halving the take's span: refused because a halved span never gets under the budget in the overlap regime and because it is K under another name, which is Edmond's own refusal. Both reasons stand, and D2 does not need a span rule: the trace itself measures where the budget was met. (ii) Splitting the batch at the root that exceeds the budget: the reason given — `trace` marks every root before it scans any, so the budget is met in the mark when no root carries a colour — is true of the *polluted prefix* (scan roots 1..*j*−1 on rows root *j*'s partial mark has already subtracted from) and is answered below; the refusal then conflates that with "mark-and-scan per root with a reset between", which is a different design with a different cost, and leaves out the third form, one reset and one redo over the prefix. (iii) "No colour of an abandoned trace is a verdict" is not reopened by a redo: the redo's trace completes. The sentence the rfc would need is "a batch's trace may be over a prefix of its roots", which is the crate's own contract already (`trace.rs`, "A bound on the roots is conservative and not a partial answer").

**Is trial deletion over a sub-batch sound when closures overlap? Proof.** Let the batch's roots be r₁..rₙ, and let S be any subset. Write M(S) for the entities the mark from S meets: M(S) is closed under the followed edges (out of the GC heap and into a mature non-candidate target the mark does not follow, `visit_child`), and M(S) ⊆ M(all). The mark subtracts exactly one per followed edge out of an *expanded* entity, into the child's row (`visit_child`: `ensure_row` then `subtract`), and every expanded entity is in M(S), so the subtractions under S are the out-edges of M(S)'s entities. Under the full batch every entity of M(S) is expanded the same way and its out-edges subtracted the same way, and further edges from M(all) ∖ M(S) into M(S) are subtracted too. Hence for every x ∈ M(S), row_S(x) ≥ row_all(x). The scan colours a row Live when its count is above zero or when it is reached from a Live row, and PotentiallyUnreachable otherwise (`scan.rs::classify_and_schedule_entity`). Claim: Live_all ∩ M(S) ⊆ Live_S. If x is Live under the full batch by count, its count under S is at least that, so it is Live under S. If x is Live under the full batch by reachability from a live y, take the path y → … → x and its last edge z → w that enters M(S) (M(S) being closed, the path stays inside once it enters). If z ∈ M(S) the whole path is inside and w = … the argument recurses to y ∈ M(S), which is Live by count under S as above. If z ∉ M(S), z is never expanded under S, so z → w is not subtracted, and row_S(w) ≥ 1: w is Live under S and reaches x inside M(S), so x is Live under S. Therefore PU_S ⊆ PU_all: every proposal a sub-batch makes, the full batch would have made. Since the full batch's proposals are themselves validated exactly by the owner, a sub-batch's are sound. What a sub-batch loses is recall on garbage whose unreachability needs subtractions from roots outside S — the shape "A↔D held only by B, B↔C garbage, A ∈ S, B ∉ S": A reads Live under S, is deferred, and is found at the turnover (bounded by N, the existing deferral bound) or when {B, C}'s teardown releases B → A and A's ring is re-registered by that decrement. This is the same loss the built collector takes today whenever K's clamp or P's room puts two roots in different batches ("a ring wider than P's room is served over several rounds", line 27–28), so D2 adds no new kind of loss.

**The polluted prefix, for completeness.** If instead the scan over r₁..r_{j−1} runs on rows root *j*'s partial mark has touched, the counts are still ≥ row_all (every subtraction made is one the full batch makes), but the Live-by-reachability argument fails: a live holder y ∈ M(r_j) ∖ M(r₁..r_{j−1}) can have been expanded and its edge into M(r₁..r_{j−1}) subtracted without y ever being scanned, so w reads PU where the full batch reads Live. The result is a *false proposal*, not an unsafe one — the owner's validation reads the external edge and answers `ExternallyReferenced` — but it is exactly a live component the mutator walks for nothing. The ruling was right to refuse this form; it is not the form D2 needs.

**Three forms, priced.** F1, *reset and redo the prefix*: on `met_its_budget()` at root *j*, `arena.reset()`, re-peek the same prefix from R (R only grows under the grant, so it is there), trace r₁..r_{j−1}, post their verdicts, `Unwalked` for r_j..rₙ, advance the whole batch. Collector time: nothing in the common case (every measured mix fits), at most twice the budget's worth on a failure (75–157 µs → 150–314 µs on the disjoint-live shape, an estimate by doubling); the redo can meet the budget again only if the graph grew under the grant, in which case everything is `Unwalked` as today. Mutator: instead of 315 rows it walks the rings past the prefix — about 8 rings of the 63 on W1's arithmetic, roughly 8 × 45,000 = 360,000 instructions against 2,872,618 (the per-ring figure is the disjoint baseline 2,853,251 / 63; an estimate). Hot path: none. Memory: none beyond today. F2, *the polluted prefix*: no second trace, false proposals in the overlap regime; refused. F3, *independent sub-batches with a reset between each*: exact per sub-batch, but a shared closure is re-traced once per sub-batch — 63 traces of the 381-member component on the overlapping shape — and cross-sub-batch garbage waits for the turnover; refused for the cost, which is the ruling's own cost objection correctly aimed. An undo log (old row word per subtraction, replayed on a met budget) would make F1's redo unnecessary at one 16-byte store per edge on every trace, and it competes with the rows for the budget on a dense closure; F1 pays only on failure and is preferred.

**Where unjudged roots go.** Back into R by the mutator's own close: `dispose_verdicts` already writes every entry it cannot dispose of back "as a registration is, its candidate bit still set", so the change is one line of `Verdict::is_root` — `Unwalked` is not a root — and the arm that already exists does the rest. The collector writes nothing into R; no premise moves. The alternative destinations are worse: a separate list is a fourth lane with a new re-offer rule and a census line to add; leaving them in P blocks P's one block for every later batch and breaks "`FREE` promises an empty P".

**What the write-back form costs and where it breaks, checked against the code.**

- *The collection over P still runs.* `Batch::is_empty` is false for verdicts without a root, so the poll opens a window, traces nothing (`walk_roots` yields no root), ends `NothingProposed`, and the close writes the entries back: about the all-live take's 15,365 instructions (line 168) rather than 2,872,618. Hot path: none.
- *R's order.* The write-back lands at R's tail behind everything registered since, so a retake traces the newer roots first and the too-big ones last; with F1 that is the right order (the ones that fit are judged, the leftovers are re-written). The ring never reads empty while a leftover stands, so `standing_since` never clears — the take repeats every interval.
- *The retake loop.* On the take path the instant restarts at the grant's end (`serve_the_grant`'s `ReleaseOnDrop`), so the cadence is the standing interval, 4 s, at 75–157 µs of collector time and about 15,000 mutator instructions per repeat: bounded cost, unbounded repetition, for as long as one closure exceeds the budget. On the threshold path — write-backs plus new registrations cross 64 — the cadence is `FALLBACK_INTERVAL_MIN`, 10 ms, and K halves per met budget: 64, 32, …, 1 in six batches, which is the partition D1 asks for, found by the built mechanism; at K = 1 a closure that still meets the budget loops every 10 ms, about 1 % of a core at 100 µs per attempt (an estimate). What stops it is the bit the fog line already named, on the hold line (11 of 64 bytes used; the line is the collector's per-visit write target): "the last batch of this mutator met the budget at its smallest span". Set by `batch` when `met_budget` and the span was 1 (threshold path) or the prefix was empty (take path); read by `serve` before the request, where it turns the take and the K=1 batch into `Served::Idle`; cleared by any batch that completes or when the mutator's note of a freeing disposition arrives (the ring changed). Under the bit the ring stands until D3.
- *The take clamp and K.* Untouched: the take still clamps at 63 and feeds K neither way; F1 posts what fit inside that span. Edmond's "a take under K" stays refused.
- *`ZeroCount` and completed deaths.* `dispose_verdicts` retires a completed death whatever its verdict, before the write-back, so an `Unwalked` root that died meanwhile is retired, not re-queued.
- *`POSTED` promising an empty P.* The close disposes of the whole prefix and advances; unchanged.
- *The deferred lane and the turnover.* A written-back root is a registration, not a deferral, so it does not pull the S60 ask; a thread with no lane keeps none. That is the point: the mutator does not walk it.

**How long is garbage past the budget held, and by what bound.** Under D2 with the bit: until the collector's attempt succeeds (D1a's doubling on the retake, up to a cap), and past the cap until D3 — memory shortage, or the collector's unavailability once D3 names it a resource. Today the pressure path fires on a refused allocation only (`collect_under_pressure`), and the memory-pressure mode thresholds "stand on borrowed numbers" (`PLAN.md`, the buffers line). So the honest bound is "until the allocator refuses", which with no cap set is never. That is the owner's line (3) taken literally, and it is a memory-for-latency trade he is making with his eyes open or not at all; it must be said in the ruling that adopts D2, not discovered in a postmortem. The one-closure-past-the-cap regime is unmeasured (silent regimes above), so nobody knows how often it is entered.

## D3 — when resources run short, the mutator collects at once

"Resources", in mechanism terms, and how each is read at the moment of decision:

**R1. Memory pressure.** The existing path: a refused allocation on the mutator raises its collecting word and waits for its token (`CollectingThread::take`). Nothing to add except D1a's reading of that word in `grow`, so that a collector's larger trace yields inside one block of rows instead of at the budget. Under D2 the mutator's pressure collection over R whole then reaches the written-back roots — line (3)'s exception, as written.

**R2. The collector not serving in time.** Read on the mutator's side: it owns R's count and its own poll cadence. The cheapest form is a counter on `MutatorCycleState` (the base block's control line the poll already touches for `signal_due`) incremented on the poll's signal branch only — the branch taken when a block of R has filled and the signal is due, so once per 8,152 registrations, never on the ordinary 7 ns poll (S60.6, 2026-09-21: 6.95–7.83 ns) — and cleared by the reading that arms the collection over P. Past N signals with no `POSTED` the mutator collects R whole in line at that poll. Cost: one load and one store on a line in cache, on a branch taken once per block; an estimate of well under a nanosecond per such poll, and zero on the others. Off Linux: nothing platform-bound. Hysteresis: N itself; a `POSTED` arriving resets it. Decision on the mutator's side, from its own words. What it does not cover is the sub-threshold standing ring, which sends no signal — that ring is D2's and the collector's, and if the collector is gone (R3) it is held; the S60 ask is the collector's too. The consequence is that a sub-threshold ring on a thread the collector never reaches is collected at pressure or exit, which is today's behaviour before S64.

**R3. No spare core.** The owner's premise: with N mutators on N cores the collector's 20–36 µs is taken from a mutator and buys 24 µs of one, a net loss; one figure (lines 211–212), one shape, never measured under contention. The signals, weighed:

| signal | what it reads | cost to read (estimate) | how often | off Linux | hysteresis | side |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `available_parallelism()` at the elder's birth | affinity mask and cgroup quota | one syscall once | once per process | std, every target | none needed | collector; with 1 core the elder is not born and every thread collects at its threshold in line |
| `CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID` against wall across a trace or a round | the ratio of CPU got to wall spent: below 1 means preempted | two `clock_gettime`, ~0.1–0.3 µs each (thread-CPU clocks are not vDSO on every kernel; an estimate) | once per batch or per round, ≥ 10 ms cadence | Windows `QueryThreadCycleTime` + `QueryPerformanceCounter` (cycles, not ns — normalise by the batch's own cycles, or use `GetThreadTimes` at 15.6 ms granularity, too coarse per batch; per round it serves); macOS/BSD have `CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID` or `thread_info` | an EWMA over rounds; enter "contended" below 0.5 for k rounds, leave above 0.9 for k rounds; k of the order of ten rounds (100 ms) | collector |
| `/proc/self/task/<tid>/schedstat` | run-queue wait in ns, directly | `open`/`read`/`close`, 1–3 µs (an estimate, unmeasured here; the owner read it on this box) | once per round | Linux only; the Windows arm would use the cycle-time ratio above | the same EWMA over the wait's share of wall | collector |
| the mutator's poll interval | none directly; a preempted mutator polls less often | nothing new | — | — | — | the wrong side: the mutator cannot tell preemption from a long statement |

The `schedstat` file gives the cleanest number — the wait itself rather than a ratio that also falls when the collector sleeps on its futex (the ratio must be taken across the trace alone, between the grant and the release, not across the round's wait). Its price is three syscalls per round, negligible at the round's cadence; its portability is nil, so the shipped form is the ratio across the trace with the Windows cycle-time arm, and `schedstat` is the instrument that calibrates it on this box. Either way the reading and the decision sit on the collector's side and touch nothing of the mutator's. What the decision *does* is the harder half: a contended collector that stops taking must hand the ring to someone, and under line (3) there is nobody — so D3 rewrites line (3) to "except under memory shortage, or when the collector is not available", which is the owner's own direction and his sentence to write (Premise changes, P1). The conveyance is one byte on the token line beside `turnover_requested` — the same line the poll's byte read already loads, so the extra load is free of a miss; read on the poll's existing slow branches (the signal branch, the lane-occupied branch) at no cost to the ordinary poll, and for the standing ring either one more load per poll (S60.6 prices two loads and a compare on touched lines at 1.1–1.3 ns) or a read every k-th poll. The mode must not flap: the byte is written only on the EWMA's crossings, and a mutator that reads "collect yourself" collects R whole at its next open-gate poll and then registers as before.

**R4. A take that met the budget.** Evidence the collector lacks the resource for this closure, read on the mutator's side from P (all `Unwalked`) — today's trigger, which D2 removes — or on the collector's side from the hold-line bit. Under D2 it is not a mutator-side resource signal at all: memory is fine, the collector tried, the roots wait for D1a's retry or for R1–R3. Treating it as a mutator trigger is what the owner's D2 forbids.

**The arm that would prove the loss.** N mutator threads pinned one per core (`sched_setaffinity` per thread; `take_perf.sh` pins the whole process to one CPU, so per-thread pinning is new), each registering rings at a fixed rate and polling, with the collector unpinned and competing; three arms — takes on, the collector retired and every mutator firing `ll_gc_collect_cycles` at its own 64th registration (in-line at the threshold, which no production path does today, so the harness has to fire it), and the collector pinned to a spare core as the control; the figure is rings reclaimed per second across all mutators and each mutator's own iteration time, at N = cores and N = cores − 1. What the harness lacks: per-thread affinity, an in-line-at-threshold mode, a throughput driver (the stress probes measure service latency, not work done per second), a process-level core cap (`taskset` or a cgroup), and a quiet box — the WSL2 readings above were taken at load 3.2–3.8. Without this arm R3 is a premise, and I would not build a mode on it.

## The worst case — the collector declines many roots

The owner's working document, `model/dev/CYCLE-SPLIT-WEAK-POINTS.md` §2.4, reads the regime from the code; here is what of it holds, what does not, and what D2 and D3 do to it.

**Cause: the algorithm's own bound, not memory — right.** `TraceScratchArena::grow` refuses the ninth block by `block_budget` and sets `budget_met`; a pool refusal is the other arm of the same function and is what `size_the_next_batch` ignores ("a pool refusal saying nothing about the batch's size"); the workspace refused at `open_for_owner` releases with no batch. The only production caller of `collect_under_pressure` is `memory::heap::entity_alloc_under_pressure` (line 2599–2600), on a refused entity allocation; there is no heap-growth threshold between "fine" and "refused". All as §2.4 says.

**K falls to one and never doubles back — right only when the closure cannot be pruned.** `size_the_next_batch` doubles on `complete` alone and halves on `met_budget`, so six met budgets take K from 64 to 1 and a seventh keeps it there. But the collector's trace prunes: its arena is opened for the owner's epoch (`open_for_owner`), and `visit_child` stops at any target whose stamp reads age ≥ 1 in that epoch and carries no candidate bit. The stamp is written by the mutator's own collection, whatever it proposed — `commit_before_drops` calls `stamp_live_components` before the early answer a commit with nothing to tear down takes (`collect.rs` line 1302), and the first stamp of a component is age 1 (`maturation.rs` line 402, `youngest.unwrap_or(0) + 1`), which is the threshold. So for a live closure of ordinary members the sequence is: batch 1 meets the budget, K = 32; the mutator's collection over P traces root 1's closure whole under no budget, reads it live, stamps it and defers root 1; batch 2's trace from its 32 roots stops one edge into the stamped core and completes; K doubles back. A `NothingProposed` collection commits nothing, so the epoch does not turn and the stamps stand until the S60 ask turns it after X, when one un-pruned trace per side happens once and the same two steps repeat. That regime costs one full trace per side per X, which is the S37/S60 price already on record, and K never reaches 1.

Where §2.4's spiral is exact is a closure the prune cannot cut: **a large live component whose members are candidates**. The rfc's own rule — "a target a queue entry names, in whichever lane that entry stands, is never pruned" (`mark.rs`, `stands_as_an_opaque_live_external` reads the flags after the stamp) — and the rule that a candidate bit is cleared only when the entity dies (`rfc`, "Speculative tracing and exact validation": "the bit is cleared only when the entity dies"; the deferred lane keeps the token) together make every long-lived object that ever took a non-final decrement a permanent candidate, cycling between R and the deferred lane once per turnover. On a heap whose live core has grown past the budget in rows — about 128,000 dense rows at 4 bytes, or 278 touched blocks at class 128, an estimate from the constants — every trace of any root in it is whole on both sides: the collector meets the budget every time, K reaches 1 and stays, and the mutator's collection over P traces the whole core once per root. §2.4's point (4) is then right and the ruling's "K buys nothing there and the take loses nothing" is wrong on the mutator's side: with K = 1 the mutator loses the sharing a 64-root collection had, and a core of c rows with r candidate roots costs r × c row visits instead of c. The prune bounds nothing in this shape, because the prune never applies to a candidate. On the corpus of 2026-08-25 (381 objects) the shape is unreachable; on a long-running process it is the population the deferral design accumulates by construction. Whether any workload gets there is unmeasured, and I would not guess.

**The rest of §2.4, checked.** The mutator's collection over P takes P's entries alone as roots: `read_batch_of_verdicts` builds `Batch { len: 0, verdicts }`, and `walk_roots` visits P's roots then the first `len` = 0 entries of R — right. R drains at one root per round: after a batch the collector's request fails at `POSTED` until the mutator's close, and the timer's minimum is 10 ms — right; the collector's cost per root is up to the budget, 75–157 µs on the sparse shape and, on a dense core, the time to fill eight blocks of rows, roughly 128,000 rows at 60–95 ns each, 8–12 ms — an estimate by multiplication. A sibling collector does not help: one token, one batch at a time — right. R grows without bound under registrations faster than the drain — right, the ring has no capacity by Y6. The end is a refused allocation and the pressure path over R whole — right, and that collection has the sharing (one trace of the core) that the 64 collections over P lost. The sub-threshold take keeps K out of it and pays the collector's time alone every interval — right.

**What D2 changes.** With `Unwalked` not a root, the spiral's mutator side disappears: each Unwalked batch costs the mutator one window and the write-back (about 15,000 instructions, the all-live figure at line 168), never a trace. F1's prefix is empty whenever the first root's closure is the core, so nothing is judged, and the hold-line bit stops the collector after its first K = 1 failure — the collector's side of the spiral, 8–12 ms per round on a dense core, ends at the first round that proves it. What remains is a standstill: the roots stand in R, judged by nobody, until D3. D2 alone therefore turns a repeated loss into a held population, and how long it is held is the composition's break 1.

**What D3 changes.** R1, pressure, is today's end of the regime and stays. R2, the unserved-signal counter, is what ends the standstill on a producing thread: N blocks of R filled with no `POSTED` and the mutator collects R whole in line — one trace of the core with the sharing intact, every live root deferred, the lane re-offered at the turnover, and the collector's next attempt after the re-offer meets the budget again and sets the bit again. So the steady state under D2 + D3 on this heap is the mutator collecting its own R whole once per N blocks or once per X, which is exactly the cost of the in-line design with no collector, plus one failed collector attempt per cycle. That is the right floor for the split to degrade to, and it is the floor §2.4 says the spiral falls below. R3, contention, changes nothing here — the collector is not contended, it is useless — but the same bit is what a contended collector would set, so the two share the mechanism. A thread that registers into a sub-threshold ring only, with an empty lane, is reached by none of R1–R3 and by no S60 ask; its ring is held until pressure or exit, as it was before S64.

**What would change the regime itself** rather than its cost: a prune that applies to candidates. That is a design question of the deferral — a deferred root proven live in this epoch is, for the collector's purposes, as opaque as a stamped non-candidate — and it is Y9's and Y12 clause 8's ground, the owner's to reopen, not this report's to propose. I name it because without it the split has a population it cannot serve, and D2 + D3 only make that population cheap to fail on.

## How D1–D3 compose, and where the composition breaks

The reconciliation to test: past the budget with memory fine, the roots stay unjudged in R (D2) and the collector gets a larger or partitioned attempt (D1a doubling on the retake; K's halving on the threshold path; F1's prefix inside every attempt); under shortage the mutator collects (D3/R1); with no collector available (R2, R3) the mutator collects at the threshold in line. Where it breaks:

1. **The one-closure regime.** A closure past the cap is retried at the standing cadence until the bit stops it, then held until R1–R3. Cost bounded, garbage unbounded in time. Must be ruled, not assumed (D2 above).
2. **"Memory is fine" has no reading.** The only pressure signal is a refused allocation; there is no high-water reading between "fine" and "refused", so the held garbage grows to the allocator's refusal. A pressure-mode threshold (`rfc/model/memory/buffers.md`, on borrowed numbers) is the missing input, and it is a memory-manager item, not a collector one.
3. **The threshold path's partition and the take's clamp disagree.** K halves to the fitting span on the threshold path; the take reads K neither way (Edmond's), so a standing ring that crossed the threshold under write-backs is partitioned and one that did not is not — F1 inside the take is what keeps the two paths at the same answer.
4. **D1a's larger window against R1.** A bigger budget without the `is_collecting` reading in `grow` lengthens the wait of a mutator short of memory; with it, the reading is on the writer line, once per block — acceptable, but it is a collector reading a mutator word, and the handshake document should record why that is not E10 again.
5. **D3's in-line mode against a standing request.** A mutator collecting in line takes `MUTATOR` over `REQUESTED|s`, which is a refusal that wakes the collector (`take_unless`); the collector's list handles it. No break, but the mode's first collection on a thread with a standing take pays the refusal's round trip.
6. **D3's mode against the standing ring.** The mode byte reaches the poll's slow branches for free and the standing ring only at one load per poll; a contended collector that stops visiting records also stops the S60 ask, so live deferred roots on a quiet thread stop being re-offered. The mode must keep the visits (cheap loads) and stop only the takes and batches.
7. **The write-back's order under F1.** Leftovers sit at the tail; a ring that grows faster than the prefix drains keeps its oldest roots forever behind new ones. Bounded by the bit (they are the ones that never fit), so a starvation of exactly the roots that cannot be judged — acceptable if said.
8. **The round's carried readings** (`PLAN.md`, "What S64 named and left"): a round whose walk reads no record carries its checkpoint's counts to the next; under D3's mode with fewer batches this shape is met more often. Close that item first.

## The concrete designs, ranked

1. **F1 — reset and redo the prefix**, inside `batch` for takes and threshold batches alike, verdicts for the prefix, `Unwalked` past it, K = max(prefix, 1) on the threshold path. Exact, no new word, no hot-path cost, collector time at most doubled on a failure. Proven by `what_a_take_costs`'s disjoint-live shape: `take_traced_batches` gains the prefix length, the census should read roots attempted ≈ 63 − prefix and rows met ≈ 5 × (63 − prefix) in the take arm; a new arm where the first root alone exceeds the budget (prefix 0, identical to today) and a hook `between_the_mark_and_the_redo` that grows the ring under the grant so the redo fails.
2. **D2's write-back and the hold-line bit** — `Unwalked` is not a root; the bit stops retakes of a ring whose smallest span met the budget; cleared by a completed batch or a freeing disposition. Proven by the same shape with F1 disabled (the test dial `budget_for_this_batch` exists): census roots attempted 0 after a take, R's count back at 63 after the close, exactly one take per interval, and none after the bit. New arm: the retake loop's cost per interval with the bit off, as the bound the ruling must quote.
3. **D3/R2 — the mutator's unserved-signal counter**, on the signal branch, collecting R whole at N signals. Proven by a case that retires the collector, registers N blocks and reads one collection fired; the poll bench (`what_the_poll_costs`) must not move.
4. **D1a — the retry budget**: doubling on a retake up to a cap, with `is_collecting` read in `grow`. Proven by the disjoint-live shape at budgets 8, 16, 32 via the existing dial, reading the collector's blocks drawn and wall against the mutator's instructions — a reading Edmond can have today, before any build.
5. **D3/R3 — the contention mode**, only after its arm; the ratio across the trace with hysteresis, the byte on the token line, `schedstat` as the calibration instrument.
6. **D1d — `ZeroCount` on an abandoned trace**; micro, one afternoon, its own case (a ring of completed deaths under a budget of one block).
7. **Sparse rows for the collector's arena** (Part B, 1) — the largest gain on W1, a representation stage.
8. **D1c — the membership handoff** — premise-level, unpriced until the phase split is read.

## Part B — beyond the fog line, at most four, ranked

1. **A sparse row form for the collector's batch (answers W1).** The budget is spent on `bytes_for(row_count)` per touched block, 2,080 bytes for one met entity in a 512-slot block and 16,408 in a 4,080-slot one; the S40.5 replay (2026-09-12) has the chunked form at 192 bytes where the flat form reserves 2,080 for two rows of class 128, and drawing one block where the flat form draws six on the loads that draw. That form was refused for the *mutator's* whole-heap collection on total bytes (717 against 762 MiB); under a *block budget* the objective is fit, not bytes, and the collector's arena is the only user. Inside the algorithm (the row form is the crate's). Cost: a second `ensure_row`/`find_initialized_row` arm, 2.1× the writes at first touch (the same entry), and the analysis document already specifies it. Arm: the census replay over the disjoint shape's touch list, then the take-cost probe with the collector's arena on the chunked form.
2. **A known sleeper is requested without a wait (answers W5).** A record whose last request expired unanswered gets its next request landed and left standing at once, as `released_unserved` already does for a released one; the cap then bounds new sleepers only. Inside the algorithm but it refines the Sage's "the consent wait stays on both paths" ruling, so it needs the cycling-pool arm that ruling itself named and nobody built.
3. **The trigger learns from the last take's yield (answers W2 in rate, not in kind).** The collector can count `ReadLive` against `Proposed` as it posts and keep a byte on the hold line; a mutator whose takes come back mostly dead is taken at a longer interval. Inside the algorithm, but it holds dead garbage longer to save the mutator 30 µs per take per 4 s — at that rate the loss W2 shows is 7.5 µs per second and not worth a dial. I would build the counter as an instrument only, until a corpus says what a standing ring holds.
4. **The contention arm (answers W4)** — D3's, above. Not an improvement, the measurement every D3 improvement waits on.

## Premise changes

- **P1. Line (3) gains a second exception.** D3's "the collector is not available" (R2, R3) lets the mutator collect R whole without shortage. It replaces nothing structural; it changes when the mutator collects, and it is the owner's own direction, his sentence to write.
- **P2. The membership handoff (D1c).** Changes the meaning of `POSTED` in the ruled handshake ("no collector holds anything; the mutator returns memory at once" becomes "withhold until the close") and gives the collector's arena a second owner. It would replace the mutator's mark and scan over `Proposed` roots, not its validation or its teardown. Not adopted here.
- **P3. The collector freeing** (the concurrent Bacon–Rajan form the owner mentions in passing): nothing in D1–D3 needs it, and the rfc's ownership invariant refuses it; named so that it is seen to be refused, not smuggled.
- **P4. Not a premise change:** `Unwalked` written back into R by the mutator's close (D2) — the mutator is R's writer and the write-back arm exists; F1's prefix inside the take — the take's clamp and K stay as Edmond ruled; the `is_collecting` hint in `grow` — a reading, not a claim; the hold-line bit — the collector's own word on its own line.
- **P5. Rulings D2 amends.** The Sage's ruling of 2026-09-22 (`DECISIONS.md` line 130): its refusal of span halving stands, its refusal of the polluted prefix stands, its "mark-and-scan per root with a reset between" objection stands against F3 and is not an objection to F1, its "shift, not a second trace" accounting does not survive, and its "Premises" sentence is true of `Proposed` and false of `Unwalked`. The rfc sentence "no colour of an abandoned trace is a verdict" stands unamended; the rfc gains "a batch's trace may be over a prefix of its roots, and the rest is unwalked".

## What I could not establish

- Whether one closure past the budget is a regime any workload enters; every measured shape fits at 8 blocks or is synthetic. Arm: none possible without a corpus; the nearest is the row cost at the smallest class.
- F1's collector cost on a failure. Arithmetic says at most twice the budget's worth; the arm is the disjoint-live shape with `take_traced_batches` reporting both passes.
